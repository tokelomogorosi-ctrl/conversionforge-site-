Chaplins Barbers: 1889 on the sign, 1889 on the site.

Case study: a traditional Sevenoaks barber at 41 Dartford Road got its first proper website, built around the 1889 heritage thread, real Google reviews, and WhatsApp booking. Single fee, handed over.

Case study

The business before

Chaplins Barbers is a traditional high-street barber at 41 Dartford Road, Sevenoaks, TN13 3TE. Skin fades, scissor cuts and hot towel shaves. The shop's own sign says established 1889, and the shop says it was voted best barber in Sevenoaks.

It had the reputation and the chairs. What it did not have was a proper web presence, which meant a first-time customer searching in Sevenoaks found less than the shop deserved.

The unnoticed detail

1889 is the year Charles Chaplin was born. The date on the sign and the name over the door tell the same story, and nobody had put that on a page.

I looked before I wrote. The whole site is built around that heritage thread, so a visitor understands the name in the first few seconds.

What was built

The shop's first proper website, at chaplinsofsevenoaks.co.uk, on a custom domain with secure hosting. Real Google reviews, rotating, so the words on the page are customers' words. A music player, because the shop has a sound. And booking by WhatsApp, the way the shop already took bookings, so nothing changed at the chair.

Handed over, not rented

This was a single-fee engagement. The domain registration was absorbed into it, ownership was transferred, and two follow-up iterations were shipped after launch to get the details right.

The shop owns the site. There is no monthly fee keeping it alive and no agency holding the keys.

What I would do next

Local search. A barber's customers search by town, and the next step is finishing the Google Business Profile and building the site's schema and locality so "barber Sevenoaks" finds Chaplins first. Then measuring bookings, not visits, because a chair filled is the only number that matters here.
