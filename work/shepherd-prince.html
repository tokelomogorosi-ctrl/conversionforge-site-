Case study

The business before

Shepherd-Prince is a family business fitting kitchens and bathrooms across Kent. Twenty-three years of fitted rooms, and the evidence of it sat in a phone: photographs of finished work, hundreds of them, seen by nobody but the person who took them.

The trade needed a page that showed the rooms at the size a customer would judge them, with a number to call.

The photographs

The client owned 1,231 unique photographs across two folders. Every candidate was looked at at full size, not as a thumbnail, and 29 were chosen.

Looking at full size is where the work was. A phone's watermark burned into the corner of a shot, and a photographer's feet at the bottom of another, were both caught and excluded at that stage. On a thumbnail sheet neither is visible; on a customer's screen both would have been.

What was built

A brand kit, so the name looks the same on the page, the van and the invoice. And a site page for the trade, with the services listed, the 29 photographs at full size, and a mobile number that goes to the person who fits the rooms.

A promise with a date on it

The agreement carried one dated obligation: the page within the first week after the first payment, once the photographs had arrived. It was met.

A date in an agreement is a promise, and I treat it as one. It goes in the calendar the day the agreement is signed, and the work is finished against it.

What I would do next

Reviews and local search. A fitter is chosen on trust, and trust online is real reviews from real customers with the town in them. The next step is a Google Business Profile with the photographs on it, the reviews gathered properly, and the page built out so "kitchen fitter" plus the town lands on Shepherd-Prince.
