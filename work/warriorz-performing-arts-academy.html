Warriorz: twenty-eight classes, finally on one page.

Case study: a Stevenage performing arts academy got a site built on its real 28-class timetable and its own photographs, with a hero film and phone-native media, live at warriorzperformingartsacademy.co.uk.

Case study

The business before

Warriorz Performing Arts Academy in Stevenage teaches dance and performing arts to children and adults. Twenty-eight classes a week, each with its own day, time and age group.

That timetable lived in the academy's booking system and in the heads of the people who ran it. A parent looking for a Tuesday class for a nine-year-old had no single page that answered the question.

What was built

A site built on the academy's real 28-class timetable, so every class has its day, time and age group on one page. The academy's own photographs throughout, and a hero film so the first thing a visitor sees is the room and the movement, not a stock image of a studio.

Live at warriorzperformingartsacademy.co.uk.

Built for the phone first

Parents look for classes on a phone, usually while doing something else. So the film that opens the site was cut for a phone screen: a portrait poster and a small portrait loop, rather than a wide film squeezed into a tall frame.

It was measured on a 390-pixel screen before and after, because a site that looks right on a laptop and wrong on a phone is wrong for most of its visitors.

What stayed the client's

The academy's existing class-booking system stayed the system of record. The site wraps it and points to it; it never replaces it. A parent books where the academy already takes bookings, and nothing at the desk changed.

That is a rule I keep. The client's working system is the truth, and the site serves it.

What I would do next

Local search, because a class is found by town. The page already carries Stevenage in the right places; the next step is the Google Business Profile finished with the booking link, and then reading Search Console to see which class names people search for, so the timetable page answers those first.
