Two search results now, not one

When someone in Sevenoaks looks for a barber, a grill or a Pilates instructor, the search happens in two places. One is the Google local pack, the map with three names under it. The other is an AI answer, in ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity, that names one or two businesses and moves on.

A site can be built to be cited by both. The same facts, laid out so a person can read them and a machine can read them too. That is the whole of what I do here, and it does not require paid ads.

The honest part first. Rankings take weeks to move, and nobody can promise you a position. What I promise is the work, the measurement, and a report you can check yourself in Search Console.

What I actually change on a site so it ranks locally

Locality goes into the titles and the headings, so a page about a Sevenoaks business says Sevenoaks where Google reads first. JSON-LD business schema carries the real address and the towns you serve. Breadcrumb schema tells a crawler where each page sits, and FAQ schema turns your real questions into results.

The sitemap gets true dates, not placeholders. The robots.txt names all 15 AI crawlers, so the assistants that answer questions are allowed to read you. Two files, llms.txt and llms-full.txt, give those assistants the plain facts about the business in your own words. Canonical tags settle which page gets the credit.

Reviews are the ones real customers wrote. I never fabricate a rating, and I will not add a star widget that the reviews do not support.

The Google Business Profile most businesses never finish

Most profiles were set up once, years ago, and left. The name is right and the phone number is right, and everything else is blank or stale.

The finished version has the right categories and the services listed one by one. It has the real photographs of the shop, the team and the work. It has weekly posts, so Google sees a business that is open. It has reviews from real customers, answered. And it carries the one link that matters most: the booking or ordering link, so a search becomes an appointment without a detour.
